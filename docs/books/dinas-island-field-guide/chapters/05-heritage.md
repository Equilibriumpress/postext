# A church beside the sea {style="page-heading"}

:::frame{slot="photo"}

::resource{id="church"}

:::

:::frame{slot="caption"}

Cwm-yr-Eglwys. Photograph: John H Darch, CC BY-SA 2.0.

:::

:::frame{slot="story"}

The surviving church wall at Cwm-yr-Eglwys marks a place where coastal history is visible in stone. The church was ruined during the storm of 1859. Today the remains face a quiet bay, a reminder of the difference between calm conditions and the force of a severe coastal storm.

Take time to view the site respectfully.

# Three things to notice {style="page-heading"}

:::module{template="note-module" name="water"}
:::frame{slot="text"}

**01 / Water**

Compare the appearance of the two bays. Wind direction and the shape of the coast influence the water you see.

:::
:::

:::module{template="note-module" name="rock"}
:::frame{slot="text"}

**02 / Rock**

Look for changes in colour and layering in the cliffs from a safe distance. Do not approach unstable ground.

:::
:::

:::module{template="note-module" name="life"}
:::frame{slot="text"}

**03 / Life**

Notice the vegetation beside the path. Coastal flowers and seabirds are seasonal; observe them without disturbance.
