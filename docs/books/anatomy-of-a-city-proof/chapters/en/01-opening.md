# A city built with water {style="display-heading"}

:::frame{slot="lead"}

Amsterdam reveals how waterways, land division and everyday movement shape an urban form.

:::

:::frame{slot="art"}

::resource{id="reguliersgracht-photo"}

Reguliersgracht, Marco Ober · CC BY-SA 4.0.

:::

:::frame{slot="story"}

Stand beside a canal and look across the water. Houses form a continuous edge, while bridges join streets that would otherwise stop. The canal has several roles: a route, a boundary between plots, a public space and a means of managing water.

**From river to port.** The Amstel provided the initial focus of the settlement. Access to the IJ connected the emerging town to regional waterways and maritime trade. Expansion required decisions about drainage, circulation and the division of land. The canal district makes the consequences of those choices visible.

:::

:::frame{slot="story-b"}

**Read the city at three scales.** At the regional scale, examine the connection between inland water and the harbour. At the district scale, trace the canals and bridges. At the scale of a single house, look at its entrance, foundation and plot.

These scales are linked. A decision about the course of a canal determines where a street runs. The street then influences where buildings face and how people reach them. Urban form is the accumulated result of such decisions, rather than a collection of isolated landmarks.

:::

:::frame{slot="concept"}

**Three scales of urban form**

City: waterways and trade. District: streets and bridges. Building: plots, entrances and foundations.

:::

# The city from above {style="display-heading"}

:::frame{slot="art"}

::resource{id="aerial"}

:::

:::frame{slot="caption"}

Aerial view of Amsterdam, 2013 · Swimmerguy269 · [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/)

:::

:::frame{slot="body-a"}

**Read the blocks.** The aerial view reveals how narrow streets and waterways enclose long urban blocks. Trace the repeated curves before looking at individual roofs. The inner district and the newer extensions differ in orientation and grain. A continuous street edge gives the water corridor a clear spatial boundary.

:::

:::frame{slot="body-b"}

**Read the connections.** Bridges punctuate the curves. The Amstel interrupts the concentric pattern, while the harbour lies beyond the northern edge of the old city. The image is an oblique photograph, not a measured plan. Use it to understand the spatial relationships, then return to the map for position and scale.

:::
