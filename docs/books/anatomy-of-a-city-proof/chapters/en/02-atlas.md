# The canal ring {style="display-heading"}

:::frame{slot="art"}

::resource{id="canal-ring"}

:::

:::frame{slot="caption"}

Original conceptual diagram. The waterways are simplified and are not suitable for navigation.

:::

:::frame{slot="right-title"}

A planned extension

:::

:::frame{slot="lead"}

A series of curved waterways surrounds the older centre and links the city to its waterfront.

:::

:::frame{slot="body-a"}

In the seventeenth century Amsterdam expanded through an organised programme of canals, streets and plots. The plan combined commercial access, housing and land development rather than treating waterways as scenery.

:::

:::frame{slot="body-b"}

The curves create long, recognisable corridors. Cross streets and bridges connect the rings. The relationship between the water and the surrounding buildings makes the overall plan visible at street level.

:::

:::frame{slot="note"}

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, canal ring property and Urban Heritage Atlas. Diagram by Equilibrium Press.
