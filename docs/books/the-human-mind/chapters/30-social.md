# Connection changes what matters
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Social · Matthew D. Lieberman
:::
:::frame{slot="idea1"}
**01 · Belonging affects wellbeing**

People monitor social belonging because relationships affect everyday safety and support. The needs of individuals are not identical.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea2"}
**02 · Rejection can feel threatening**

Rejection can produce a strong social response. Comparing it with physical pain requires care: shared measures do not mean identical experiences.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea3"}
**03 · Perspective taking requires work**

Perspective taking uses inferences about another person's thoughts. Accuracy improves when those inferences are checked in conversation.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea4"}
**04 · Social learning changes behavior**

Learning from others reduces the need to test every action alone. Social learning also spreads misinformation.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea5"}
**05 · Status shapes interactions**

Status and recognition influence interactions. They are part of the context, not complete explanations of behavior.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea6"}
**06 · Mentalizing helps cooperation**

Mentalizing supports cooperation by making goals and intentions thinkable. It does not guarantee we interpret another person correctly.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea7"}
**07 · Identity emerges through relationships**

Relationships contribute to how people describe themselves. No one encounter or group membership defines the entirety of a person.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea8"}
**08 · Brain findings require measured claims**

Neuroscience helps investigate social processes but cannot resolve every social question by a brain image. Behavior and context still matter.
:::
:::frame{slot="practicehead"}
## Two things to try
:::
:::frame{slot="practice1"}
**09 · Check a guess about another person**

Ask a colleague what they hoped to accomplish in a recent decision. Compare it with your guess.
:::
:::frame{slot="practice2"}
**10 · Plan regular meaningful contact**

Invite someone you value to one recurring conversation or shared activity. Respect their answer.
