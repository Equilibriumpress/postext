# When there is not enough
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Scarcity · Sendhil Mullainathan and Eldar Shafir
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Scarcity narrows focus**

An urgent shortage can dominate thought. Attention to the immediate problem grows while less pressing responsibilities receive less space.

**02 · Tunneling hides other needs**

Attention to an urgent bill may crowd out an important form or appointment. The overlooked task is not proof that it matters less.

**03 · Bandwidth is limited**

Planning and monitoring consume mental capacity. Scarcity burdens should not be mistaken for a person's permanent intelligence or character.

**04 · Short-term fixes create trade-offs**

A short-term solution may increase future costs. Borrowing time or money from tomorrow sometimes creates a repeating cycle.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Time poverty affects choices**

Time scarcity shapes small decisions as much as financial scarcity does. A packed calendar leaves fewer opportunities for correction.

**06 · Slack protects resilience**

Slack means breathing room in a system, not laziness. Spare time and funds help absorb predictable disruptions.

**07 · Simpler systems lower cognitive load**

A simpler form or fewer deadlines can reduce cognitive demands. Improving a service may matter more than telling individuals to organize better.

**08 · Structural conditions matter**

The causes of scarcity include resources, institutions and social conditions. Individual self-help alone cannot resolve structural shortages.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Build a realistic deadline buffer**

Identify one recurring deadline and add a realistic time buffer if circumstances permit.

**10 · Simplify one administrative step**

Find a repeated form or deadline that burdens users. Simplify one requirement within your control.
