# Why moral disagreements persist
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
The Righteous Mind · Jonathan Haidt
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Intuition precedes reasons**

Moral intuition often comes before a consciously prepared reason. The rider-and-elephant metaphor is a way to discuss this tension.

**02 · Moral priorities differ**

People may emphasize care, fairness, loyalty or authority differently in one dispute. The same person can weigh these concerns differently elsewhere.

**03 · Group identity affects argument**

Political and social groups reinforce shared explanations. Belonging can affect which evidence feels credible.

**04 · Fairness has multiple meanings**

Fairness is interpreted through competing ideas, including equality and proportionality. A debate may use one word for different priorities.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Disgust influences some reactions**

Emotional reactions can influence moral judgment without settling whether the judgment is justified. Reflection remains necessary.

**06 · Community and autonomy compete**

Communities weigh individual autonomy against collective commitments in different ways. Neither is captured by one simple score.

**07 · Listening can reveal values**

Ask which harm or obligation a person sees before debating solutions. The answer helps explain the concern without making it correct by default.

**08 · Pluralism needs careful interpretation**

Moral-foundations research has critics and competing interpretations. Avoid using the model to classify people as fixed moral types.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Name the value behind disagreement**

Ask each speaker which concern matters most. Record their words before interpreting them.

**10 · Present the opposing position fairly**

Restate the other person's reason and ask whether they recognize it as their position.
