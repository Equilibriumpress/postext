# How a self becomes an experience
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Being You · Anil Seth
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Perception involves prediction**

Seth describes perception as an active interpretation of sensory causes. What appears immediately given is partly inferred.

**02 · The body informs experience**

Internal bodily signals contribute to experience alongside information from the world. Their interpretation does not reveal a single fixed sense of self.

**03 · Consciousness has many features**

Conscious experience has several dimensions, including awareness of the world and of oneself. A simple on-off switch is inadequate.

**04 · A sense of self is constructed**

The feeling of being a stable self may arise from coordinated body and brain processes. This is a theoretical account, not a final explanation.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Brain theories are contested**

Researchers disagree about which brain processes are necessary for consciousness. No single popular model has settled the debate.

**06 · Illusions reveal perception limits**

Illusions show that perception can be coherent while differing from external measurements. Error reveals how interpretations are made.

**07 · Attention differs from awareness**

Attention to a signal and conscious experience of that signal are not identical concepts. Their relation remains an open research question.

**08 · Uncertainty remains fundamental**

Much about subjective experience remains unexplained. A scientific account should identify its open questions alongside its evidence.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Observe perception in two contexts**

View one ambiguous picture with two different captions. Record how each changes what you notice.

**10 · Separate sensation from meaning**

Describe one familiar bodily sensation, then write two possible meanings for it.
