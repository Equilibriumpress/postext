# Thinking outside the skull
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
The Extended Mind · Annie Murphy Paul
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Movement changes cognitive experience**

Physical movement changes arousal and attention. It may help a person think through a problem, without guaranteeing a solution.

**02 · Gesture can support explanation**

A diagram makes relationships available to inspect and revise. The shapes are useful only if their labels and assumptions remain open to challenge.

**03 · Physical spaces cue thoughts**

A room's layout provides cues and constraints. Spaces support different kinds of thinking when their purpose is clear.

**04 · Tools externalize complex tasks**

External notes and diagrams keep intermediate steps visible. This frees working memory for comparing and evaluating possibilities.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Groups distribute knowledge**

A group can distribute knowledge among members. Shared thinking still requires coordination and careful evaluation of claims.

**06 · Natural environments affect attention**

Natural settings sometimes restore attention following demanding work. Effects depend on the environment and individual.

**07 · Sketching makes ideas manipulable**

Other people contribute knowledge and alternative perspectives. Group work also brings conformity, so ask who has not yet been heard.

**08 · Collaborative thinking needs structure**

Collaboration is most useful when contributions and dissent are welcomed. A unanimous group is not automatically more accurate.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Draw a decision's possible paths**

Sketch three possible actions and one consequence of each. Circle the missing information.

**10 · Test an idea by changing perspective**

Explain the sketch to someone else and record one question their perspective adds.
