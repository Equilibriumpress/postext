# Autonomy, mastery and purpose
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Drive · Daniel H. Pink
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Control can weaken intrinsic interest**

Close supervision and contingent rewards can narrow attention in some tasks. Their effects vary by context, complexity and timing.

**02 · Choice promotes ownership**

A meaningful choice about method can support ownership when goals remain clear. Unlimited freedom without support is not the same as autonomy.

**03 · Skill grows through practice**

Mastery develops through practice at a manageable level of difficulty. Progress involves effort and useful information about mistakes.

**04 · Purpose gives direction**

A meaningful purpose connects a task to a contribution. A slogan is insufficient when the task itself conflicts with stated aims.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Rewards differ by task**

Rewards can support some routine activities but crowd out interest in others. Avoid treating every kind of work as identical.

**06 · Feedback guides improvement**

Timely, specific feedback identifies what to improve. Praise without information is a weak substitute for learning.

**07 · Mastery requires persistence**

Mastery depends on feedback and repeated attempts, not a constant feeling of progress. Build time to improve a skill into the work itself.

**08 · Supportive settings enable autonomy**

A workplace can offer choices, development and a clear reason for work. Organizational constraints determine how much room is available.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Offer a meaningful choice of method**

For one team task, agree on the goal and let the worker choose between two workable methods.

**10 · Track improvement with feedback**

Pick one skill in the task and agree on a specific feedback point before the next attempt.
