BOOK 06 / THE IDEAS
:::
:::frame{slot="title"}
# Learning gets stronger when recall takes work
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Make It Stick · Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III and Mark A. McDaniel
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Pull knowledge from memory**

Trying to answer without notes strengthens later retrieval. A short quiz used for practice is different from a high-pressure assessment.

**02 · Space the repetitions**

Space attempts over time instead of repeating everything at once. Compare recall after a delay, not only how confident you feel at the end of study.

**03 · Interleave related skills**

Switching between problem types makes practice feel harder. The learner must identify the appropriate method instead of repeating one routine.

**04 · Fluency can mislead**

Freshly reread text feels familiar, yet that feeling does not demonstrate future recall. Test comprehension away from the page.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Desirable difficulty has limits**

Effort sometimes improves retention when a learner has adequate foundations and feedback. Overwhelming confusion is not a learning strategy.

**06 · Feedback repairs mistakes**

Checking an attempted answer identifies misconceptions. Feedback works best when it tells the learner how to improve the next attempt.

**07 · Explain links in your own words**

Explain a new idea without copying a source sentence. Then check whether your explanation connects the right causes, steps and examples.

**08 · Reflect after practice**

Describe the strategy used, what failed and what to try next. This makes a lesson more useful across unfamiliar tasks.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Close the book, then retrieve**

Close a short reading and write three answers from memory. Reopen the page to correct gaps.

**10 · Space a second and third attempt**

Schedule one recall tomorrow and another several days later. Record what was still difficult.
