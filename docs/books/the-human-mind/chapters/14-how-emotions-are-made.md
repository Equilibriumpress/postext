# What makes a feeling an emotion?
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
How Emotions Are Made · Lisa Feldman Barrett
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Emotions are not simple readouts**

Barrett proposes that an emotion is constructed in a particular situation. This contrasts with accounts centered on fixed emotion programs.

**02 · Prediction has a role**

Bodily sensations are information, but a racing heart has many possible causes. Context helps a person interpret what they notice.

**03 · Body signals contribute**

Heartbeat, breathing and other bodily changes inform the experience of feeling. A sensation alone does not uniquely identify an emotion.

**04 · Context shifts meaning**

A racing heart might accompany anticipation, exertion or fear. The surrounding circumstances help a person interpret the sensation.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Concepts shape categories**

People learn words and categories for experiences. Those concepts influence how they label, recognize and remember emotional episodes.

**06 · Faces need interpretation**

A facial expression cannot always be translated into one emotion without context. People and cultures vary in expressive behavior.

**07 · Different theories coexist**

An emotion label reflects learned concepts and circumstances. Different labels do not by themselves establish different underlying brain mechanisms.

**08 · Description expands choices**

Separating bodily signals, thoughts and surroundings creates a more precise vocabulary. A description need not dictate the next action.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Observe before labeling a feeling**

List two bodily sensations and three details of the situation before choosing an emotion word.

**10 · Compare two possible interpretations**

Offer two explanations for the sensation. Note which details support each and what remains unknown.
