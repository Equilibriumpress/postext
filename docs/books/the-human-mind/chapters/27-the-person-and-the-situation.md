# The setting changes the story
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
The Person and the Situation · Lee Ross and Richard E. Nisbett
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Context changes action**

A person may act differently in two settings. Explaining the difference requires knowledge of both circumstances, not a personality label alone.

**02 · Dispositions are not destiny**

A quiet meeting does not prove a quiet personality. Compare behavior across settings before treating one observation as a lasting trait.

**03 · Observers underrate circumstances**

Observers often give too much weight to character and too little to circumstances. This error is especially tempting when the context is hidden.

**04 · Social roles shift decisions**

Assigned roles change what behavior is rewarded or permitted. A role does not erase the responsibility for the action.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Norms create local expectations**

Local norms signal what others expect. A behavior that appears personal may also follow a routine of the group.

**06 · Small differences matter**

Small changes to choices or surroundings can alter results. An apparently minor instruction may have practical consequences.

**07 · Perception filters explanations**

Observers often notice a person's action before the constraints around it. Ask which parts of the setting the observer could not see.

**08 · Prediction needs situational details**

Predicting behavior improves when we ask what constraints and opportunities someone faced. Labels alone contain little situational information.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · List constraints before judging**

Before judging a late task, name three possible constraints and which could be verified.

**10 · Change a meeting condition**

Provide preparation time or a smaller discussion group, then compare participation.
