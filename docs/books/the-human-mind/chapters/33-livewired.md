# A brain built to keep changing
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Livewired · David Eagleman
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Experience affects neural organization**

Circuits change in response to activity, learning and sensory input. The brain is an adaptive biological system, not a fixed wiring plan.

**02 · Adaptation remains possible**

Improvement after practice shows adaptation in behavior. Measuring neural reorganization requires independent observations, not an inference from skill alone.

**03 · Practice alters efficiency**

Repeated tasks can change which networks are recruited and how well they coordinate. A new skill still needs time and feedback.

**04 · Sensory systems can reorganize**

When input patterns change, neural processing may respond. Sensory adaptation is not unlimited, and outcomes differ between people.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Technology can change input**

Eagleman explores devices that translate information into new sensory signals. Learning to use a signal does not guarantee new biological senses.

**06 · The brain uses ongoing feedback**

Incoming information informs future responses. Feedback links an attempted action with what the person subsequently learns.

**07 · Flexibility has constraints**

Age, injury, available practice and biology affect adaptation. Plasticity describes possibilities under constraints rather than unlimited change.

**08 · Individual paths differ**

Two people may develop different strategies under similar demands. Avoid drawing conclusions from a single demonstration or clinical story.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Track progress with feedback**

Practise one small skill for a week. Record an observable outcome after each attempt.

**10 · Try a different learning cue**

Repeat the same task with a different cue. Compare performance without inferring brain changes.
