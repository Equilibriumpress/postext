# Feelings are information, not instructions
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Emotional Agility · Susan David
:::
:::frame{slot="idea1"}
**01 · Emotions communicate concerns**

A feeling may signal an unmet need or a concern, but it does not dictate the right action. Listen before choosing a response.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea2"}
**02 · Naming feelings creates room**

Naming a feeling precisely creates a moment for reflection. It does not require suppressing the feeling or accepting every thought attached to it.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea3"}
**03 · Thoughts are not commands**

A thought can arrive uninvited without being an order. Describing it as a thought creates useful distance from its content.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea4"}
**04 · Avoidance can narrow behavior**

Pushing uncomfortable emotions away can make them harder to approach. The aim is willingness, not endless analysis or forced positivity.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea5"}
**05 · Values guide selected actions**

Values are enduring directions, unlike passing moods. A small action can express a value even during a difficult day.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea6"}
**06 · Self-compassion supports change**

Responding kindly to a setback avoids turning a mistake into a fixed judgment about character. Accountability still matters.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea7"}
**07 · Perspective changes emotional grip**

A values-based action may still feel uncomfortable. Judge the step by whether it serves a meaningful aim, not by immediate relief.
:::
:::frame{slot="idea8"}
**08 · Small commitments sustain movement**

A change that matters need not start with a dramatic promise. Repeated manageable steps test what works in ordinary conditions.
:::
:::frame{slot="practicehead"}
## Two things to try
:::
:::frame{slot="practice1"}
**09 · Name the feeling without obeying it**

Write one precise emotion word and the impulse it creates. Pause before acting on that impulse.
:::
:::frame{slot="practice2"}
**10 · Choose a small values-based action**

Name one value in the situation, then complete a five-minute action that supports it.
