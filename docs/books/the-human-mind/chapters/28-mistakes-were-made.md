# Why admitting error hurts
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Mistakes Were Made (But Not by Me) · Carol Tavris and Elliot Aronson
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Dissonance motivates explanations**

An action that conflicts with a person's self-image creates discomfort. A convenient explanation may restore consistency without correcting the mistake.

**02 · Commitment hardens a position**

Each public defense can make retreat feel more costly. Reassess the remaining evidence without treating earlier statements as binding.

**03 · Memory supports self-image**

Memory tends to support the story we tell about ourselves. Repeated retelling may strengthen that interpretation rather than its accuracy.

**04 · Groups defend shared identity**

A shared identity makes criticism feel personal. Groups can defend a position even when it harms the original goal.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Escalation hides sunk costs**

Sunk costs are unrecoverable, but they often shape the next decision. Ask what you would choose if starting today.

**06 · Admission can repair errors**

An honest admission describes what happened and what will change. It is different from performing guilt without repair.

**07 · Humility aids correction**

A correction is easier to make when it does not require a complete public humiliation. Separate the error from a judgment about character.

**08 · Evidence should outrank pride**

A record made before outcomes are known can expose retrospective excuses. Evidence should remain available after the result.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Set a rule for revising a choice**

Before committing, record the evidence that would make you reconsider the plan.

**10 · Admit an error and plan a repair**

Name one mistaken choice and write one repair step without explaining away the evidence.
