# What changes a deeply held belief?
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
How Minds Change · David McRaney
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Facts alone seldom shift identity beliefs**

Facts can be dismissed when they threaten a group identity. More information is not automatically more influence.

**02 · A question invites reflection**

Ask a sincere question before supplying a correction. A question helps reveal the premise behind a belief, but the response still needs evidence.

**03 · Curiosity is better than accusation**

Direct accusations prompt people to defend themselves. An open-ended conversation gives room for uncertainty.

**04 · People explain their own reasoning**

When people describe their own reasoning, they sometimes spot a contradiction themselves. The insight belongs to the speaker.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Social belonging shapes opinion**

Belonging influences which sources seem trustworthy. A factual correction rarely addresses the social cost of switching sides.

**06 · Respect reduces defensive reactions**

Respectful listening does not require agreement. It lowers the chance that a disagreement becomes a contest over dignity.

**07 · Listen for underlying values**

A disagreement about facts may express concern about dignity, safety or fairness. Identify the concern before arguing about the interpretation of evidence.

**08 · Changes often take multiple encounters**

Some changes occur through many experiences rather than one decisive conversation. Do not measure success by immediate conversion.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Ask how someone formed a view**

Ask someone when an opinion first mattered to them. Reflect their answer before giving yours.

**10 · Find the question behind a dispute**

Write the disputed question in terms both speakers accept before arguing about answers.
