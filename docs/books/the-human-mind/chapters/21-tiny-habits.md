# The smallest reliable action
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Tiny Habits · BJ Fogg
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Ability interacts with motivation**

The Fogg Behavior Model brings motivation, ability and a prompt into one moment. If an action never happens, examine all three.

**02 · Prompt design matters**

A prompt must appear when the action is feasible. Put the new step after an existing event rather than depending on a vague intention.

**03 · Tiny actions reduce resistance**

An action as small as opening a notebook removes much of the resistance to starting. The small step is a beginning, not a full outcome.

**04 · Celebration marks success**

A positive feeling at the end of a completed behavior can support repetition. The celebration should feel sincere rather than forced.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Anchors supply predictable timing**

Anchoring a new behavior to an event already in the day provides a cue. A vague plan lacks that reliable place.

**06 · Growth starts with repetition**

Repeated success can make a routine more available. Growth depends on whether the behavior remains useful in the person's context.

**07 · Behavior design needs experiments**

If the routine fails, change its timing, size or prompt and observe the result. A failed design is information, not a measure of character.

**08 · Adjust the behavior to real life**

An effective routine fits real constraints. A behavior that works during a quiet week may need resizing during a demanding month.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Choose a ten-second action**

Choose an action under ten seconds, such as opening a notebook. Try only that small step.

**10 · Anchor an action to a reliable event**

After a daily event such as switching on the kettle, do one tiny action and acknowledge it.
