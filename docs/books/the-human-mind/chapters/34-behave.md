# No action has only one cause
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
Behave · Robert M. Sapolsky
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Immediate brain activity shapes action**

Neural activity immediately before an act helps determine what occurs. That activity reflects earlier conditions rather than appearing from nowhere.

**02 · Hormones act within contexts**

Hormones act in contexts shaped by experience and other signals. No single hormone level explains a person's complex action.

**03 · Prior experience shifts response**

Earlier learning changes which responses become available under stress. Previous experience interacts with the current environment.

**04 · Development matters**

Development affects later opportunities, vulnerabilities and skills. It does not provide a deterministic biography of adult behavior.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Genes interact with environment**

Genes influence biology while environments influence how predispositions unfold. A gene-to-behavior slogan misses the interaction.

**06 · Culture shapes behavior**

Culture supplies shared practices, norms and institutional expectations. These inputs operate over years as well as moments.

**07 · Stress affects judgment**

Stress can narrow a person's options and alter judgment. It neither explains every reaction nor removes the need to repair harm.

**08 · Single-cause stories mislead**

Biological, developmental and cultural explanations fit together across timescales. A one-factor story usually leaves important evidence out.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Trace one action across timescales**

For one everyday action, note the immediate event, recent conditions and longer-term context.

**10 · Question one character label**

Swap a quick personality label for two possible causes and the evidence needed to assess them.
