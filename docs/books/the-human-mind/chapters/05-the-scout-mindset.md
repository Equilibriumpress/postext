# Searching for what is true
:::
:::frame{slot="metadata"}
The Scout Mindset · Julia Galef
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**01 · Defense of identity shapes belief**

Treating an argument as a battle rewards defending a position. Galef's scout metaphor encourages an accurate map of the evidence.

**02 · Accuracy is a learnable aim**

Accuracy grows through repeated checks: name a claim, estimate your confidence, then compare the estimate with new observations rather than rehearsing certainty.

**03 · Update beliefs with new evidence**

An honest update might be small rather than a complete reversal. Name the piece of evidence that altered your confidence.

**04 · Notice motivated reasoning**

Motivated reasoning selects evidence for a preferred result. Ask whether the same reasoning would persuade you from the other side.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**05 · Calibration beats certainty**

Well-calibrated confidence distinguishes a strong hypothesis from a certainty. Expressing a probability reveals uncertainty more clearly.

**06 · Emotional responses reveal stakes**

Defensiveness is informative: a topic may threaten identity or belonging. Recognizing this feeling helps separate the self from the claim.

**07 · Ask what would change your mind**

Specify what observation would lower your confidence before you search. If no possible evidence counts, the belief has become difficult to test.

**08 · Disagreement can generate information**

An opposing view may contain a fact you have not considered. Curiosity is more productive than treating disagreement as a personal verdict.
:::
:::frame{slot="applications"}
## Two things to try

**09 · Name a belief you would revise**

Write one belief, your confidence from 0 to 100 and one observation that would challenge it.

**10 · Update confidence after new evidence**

Read one credible contrary source, then record whether your confidence changes and why.
