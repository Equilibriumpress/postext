# The Big Six
:::
:::frame{slot="intro"}
Part of the fun of visiting Singapore lies in taking in the eateries, markets and places of worship offered up by its diverse ethnic districts. But the island harbours several landmark tourist attractions that you really must not miss. Together, they reflect history, nature, ethnic culture and cuisine.

# Eating · Chinatown
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**Maxwell Food Centre**

Junction of South Bridge Rd and Maxwell Rd. An old-style hawker centre, a stone's throw from the centre of Chinatown.

**Mitzi's**

24–26 Murray Terrace. The cracking Cantonese food in this simple place draws crowds, so be prepared to queue.

**Moi Kong Hakka Restaurant**

22 Murray Terrace. The food of the semi-nomadic Hakka tribe relies heavily on preserved ingredients.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**Moti Mahal Restaurant**

18 Murray St. One of Singapore's best-known Indian restaurants, serving murg massalam.

**Mouth Restaurant**

Chinatown Point, 133 New Bridge Rd. A Hong Kong dim sum menu.

**Swee Kee**

12 Ann Siang Rd. A Cantonese restaurant with a long history of fish-head noodles.

# Eating · Boat Quay
:::
:::frame{slot="left"}
**Hot Stones**

53 Boat Quay. Hot stones represent the unusual twist at this restaurant: steaks, chicken and seafood are grilled on slabs of Alpine rock.

**Louis' Oyster Bar**

36 Boat Quay. Oysters and seafood, named for Louis Armstrong.

**Our Village**

5th floor, 46 Boat Quay. Indian and Sri Lankan food with views of the river.
:::
:::frame{slot="right"}
**Sukhothai**

47 Boat Quay. Thai food with riverside tables.

**Town Restaurant**

Fullerton Hotel, 1 Fullerton Square. Asian and Western dishes, with meals from the barbecue.

**Boat Quay**

Restaurants and bars line the river at the edge of Singapore's CBD.
