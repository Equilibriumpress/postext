# THE HUMAN MIND {#introduction}

30 Books · 300 Ideas · One Visual Guide

A person calls a familiar face by the wrong name. A judge reaches a decision that another judge would reject. A learner feels ready after rereading a page but struggles to explain it the next morning. These moments pose different questions about the same human mind: what do we notice, remember, feel and decide, and what do we miss?

This guide introduces thirty influential books that approach those questions from different directions. It is organized as six conversations rather than a ranking of authors. Each book receives one independent visual interpretation, eight connected insights and two practical exercises. The illustrations make mechanisms visible, while brief scenes give the ideas a recognizable setting.

Some scenes refer to documented cases or demonstrations; the others are invented editorial examples, clearly labelled. A plausible example is not evidence for a psychological theory. Where a scientific model is disputed, the account should name its limits rather than present a neat picture as settled fact. Readers should consult the original books and the linked research for the full arguments.

Begin with a question you care about. Follow the differences between authors and test a practical prompt when appropriate. The purpose is not to diagnose a mind or promise personal transformation. It is to leave each spread knowing what a particular author contributes, why the question matters and which uncertainty remains.
