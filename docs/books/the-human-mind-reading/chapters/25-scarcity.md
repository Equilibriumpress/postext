# 20. Scarcity {#rl-scarcity}

*Sendhil Mullainathan and Eldar Shafir · 2013 · Habits & Motivation*

**Shortage captures attention and creates difficult trade-offs.**

::resource{id="thm-scarcity"}

## In context

*ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO*

A parent working two jobs receives a school form that must be returned quickly. A changed shift absorbs attention and the form goes unanswered. A new reminder arrives, adding another task to an already crowded week. The missed deadline does not reveal laziness or poor values; the example shows how constraints multiply.

## What distinguishes this book

Sendhil Mullainathan and Eldar Shafir describe how limited time and money can narrow attention around the urgent need. Their major contribution is to shift the question from individual discipline toward the cognitive demands of persistent scarcity.

## What the claim does not establish

Scarcity has economic and institutional causes. A checklist, buffer or reminder may help with one demand, but does not substitute for adequate income, time and access to services.

> **Read alongside Drive.** Pink describes autonomy as a condition that supports motivation. Scarcity asks what happens when the practical room to choose shrinks. The reader should not interpret constrained choices as a failure to want the right things.

## Eight ideas

1. **Scarcity narrows focus** An urgent shortage can dominate thought. Attention to the immediate problem grows while less pressing responsibilities receive less space.

2. **Tunneling hides other needs** Attention to an urgent bill may crowd out an important form or appointment. The overlooked task is not proof that it matters less.

3. **Bandwidth is limited** Planning and monitoring consume mental capacity. Scarcity burdens should not be mistaken for a person's permanent intelligence or character.

4. **Short-term fixes create trade-offs** A short-term solution may increase future costs. Borrowing time or money from tomorrow sometimes creates a repeating cycle.

5. **Time poverty affects choices** Time scarcity shapes small decisions as much as financial scarcity does. A packed calendar leaves fewer opportunities for correction.

6. **Slack protects resilience** Slack means breathing room in a system, not laziness. Spare time and funds help absorb predictable disruptions.

7. **Simpler systems lower cognitive load** A simpler form or fewer deadlines can reduce cognitive demands. Improving a service may matter more than telling individuals to organize better.

8. **Structural conditions matter** The causes of scarcity include resources, institutions and social conditions. Individual self-help alone cannot resolve structural shortages.

## Two things to try

1. **Build a realistic deadline buffer** Look for a recurring deadline that tends to become urgent. Where you have discretion, add a workable buffer or earlier reminder, and note any constraint you cannot control.

2. **Simplify one administrative step** Review one process used by people with limited time. Identify a question, step or repeated document that adds avoidable work and remove or simplify it if you have authority.
