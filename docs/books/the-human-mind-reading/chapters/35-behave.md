# 28. Behave {#rl-behave}

*Robert M. Sapolsky · 2017 · The Brain & The Self*

**Trace influences from a second ago to a lifetime before judging behavior.**

::resource{id="thm-behave"}

## In context

*ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO*

After poor sleep, a student snaps at a classmate. The comment is immediate, but the circumstances include a difficult week, expectations about conflict and a shared classroom culture. Considering those influences makes the event more understandable without deciding that the remark was harmless.

## What distinguishes this book

Robert Sapolsky traces behavior through interacting causes across moments, hormones, development and social environments. His distinctive approach is to refuse a single-level explanation of why someone acted as they did.

## What the claim does not establish

A hypothetical timeline cannot identify the causes of any individual behavior. Biological explanation is not diagnosis, prediction or permission to harm another person.

> **Read alongside The Person and the Situation.** Ross and Nisbett show how a setting can reshape what an observer attributes to personality. Sapolsky extends the inquiry across many biological and developmental timescales. Neither argument makes a single behavior inevitable.

## Eight ideas

1. **Immediate brain activity shapes action** Neural activity immediately before an act helps determine what occurs. That activity reflects earlier conditions rather than appearing from nowhere.

2. **Hormones act within contexts** Hormones act in contexts shaped by experience and other signals. No single hormone level explains a person's complex action.

3. **Prior experience shifts response** Earlier learning changes which responses become available under stress. Previous experience interacts with the current environment.

4. **Development matters** Development affects later opportunities, vulnerabilities and skills. It does not provide a deterministic biography of adult behavior.

5. **Genes interact with environment** Genes influence biology while environments influence how predispositions unfold. A gene-to-behavior slogan misses the interaction.

6. **Culture shapes behavior** Culture supplies shared practices, norms and institutional expectations. These inputs operate over years as well as moments.

7. **Stress affects judgment** Stress can narrow a person's options and alter judgment. It neither explains every reaction nor removes the need to repair harm.

8. **Single-cause stories mislead** Biological, developmental and cultural explanations fit together across timescales. A one-factor story usually leaves important evidence out.

## Two things to try

1. **Trace one action across timescales** Choose a manageable behavior and record what happened seconds before it, recent pressures and possible longer-term influences. Mark which observations are facts and which are guesses.

2. **Question one character label** Take a claim such as 'he is careless' and rewrite it as two testable explanations involving circumstances or prior experience. List what information would distinguish them.
