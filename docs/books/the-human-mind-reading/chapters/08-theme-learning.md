# Memory & Learning {#theme-learning}

A student rereads a page three times and underlines the important sentences. During a conversation the next day, the explanation will not come. A colleague who tested herself without notes remembers more, though practice felt less comfortable.

The feeling of fluency is not the same as durable learning. Remembering relies on attention and retrieval, while understanding also depends on what a learner already knows.

Make It Stick examines retrieval and spaced practice. Remember asks how memories are formed and lost. The Organized Mind considers where attention goes when demands compete. The Extended Mind broadens cognition to useful external support. Willingham brings background knowledge and instruction into the picture. These are complementary, not interchangeable theories of learning.

## Five connected readings

- :ref{id="rl-make-it-stick" text="Make It Stick"} — Retrieval practice tests what persists after the page is closed.
- :ref{id="rl-remember" text="Remember"} — A memory failure may begin when attention is divided at encoding.
- :ref{id="rl-the-organized-mind" text="The Organized Mind"} — External reminders protect limited attention but need maintenance.
- :ref{id="rl-the-extended-mind" text="The Extended Mind"} — Diagrams and surroundings sometimes support complex reasoning.
- :ref{id="rl-why-dont-students-like-school" text="Why Don't Students Like School?"} — Teaching examples should start from the learner's existing knowledge.

**A necessary distinction.** Useful study techniques do not replace prior knowledge, feedback or conditions that make sustained attention possible.

**Carry this question forward:** What would someone remember about your explanation tomorrow, with the page closed?
