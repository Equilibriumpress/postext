# 09. The Extended Mind {#rl-the-extended-mind}

*Annie Murphy Paul · 2021 · Memory & Learning*

**People solve problems with bodies, spaces and relationships.**

::resource{id="thm-the-extended-mind"}

## In context

*ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO*

A museum team debates how visitors will move through a new exhibition. Spoken suggestions remain vague. When members walk the rooms, sketch routes and mark bottlenecks, they notice conflicts that the meeting had missed. The drawing becomes a working tool, not a guarantee of a better design.

## What distinguishes this book

Annie Murphy Paul argues that thought is shaped and supported by bodies, spaces, tools and other people. Her distinct contribution is to treat the surroundings as active resources for reasoning rather than merely as distractions to remove.

## What the claim does not establish

The broader claim that cognition extends beyond the brain is debated. An external diagram can expose a problem or introduce a new mistake, depending on its assumptions and use.

> **Read alongside The Organized Mind.** Levitin's organizational systems can keep information available. Paul asks what happens when a person actively thinks with a sketch, a gesture or a conversation. Offloading a reminder is different from exploring an idea through a tool.

## Eight ideas

1. **Movement changes cognitive experience** Physical movement changes arousal and attention. It may help a person think through a problem, without guaranteeing a solution.

2. **Gesture can support explanation** A diagram makes relationships available to inspect and revise. The shapes are useful only if their labels and assumptions remain open to challenge.

3. **Physical spaces cue thoughts** A room's layout provides cues and constraints. Spaces support different kinds of thinking when their purpose is clear.

4. **Tools externalize complex tasks** External notes and diagrams keep intermediate steps visible. This frees working memory for comparing and evaluating possibilities.

5. **Groups distribute knowledge** A group can distribute knowledge among members. Shared thinking still requires coordination and careful evaluation of claims.

6. **Natural environments affect attention** Natural settings sometimes restore attention following demanding work. Effects depend on the environment and individual.

7. **Sketching makes ideas manipulable** Other people contribute knowledge and alternative perspectives. Group work also brings conformity, so ask who has not yet been heard.

8. **Collaborative thinking needs structure** Collaboration is most useful when contributions and dissent are welcomed. A unanimous group is not automatically more accurate.

## Two things to try

1. **Draw a decision's possible paths** On paper, map three realistic options for a decision. Add one likely consequence and one unknown under each, then circle the question that would help you choose.

2. **Test an idea by changing perspective** Show your decision sketch to a colleague or friend and ask what assumption it leaves out. Write down their question and verify it independently before changing your choice.
